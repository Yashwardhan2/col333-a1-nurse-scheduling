1. PROBLEM FORMULATION

The roster is modelled as a CSP with N*D variables, one per (nurse, day), over
the domain {M, A, E, R, B}. The constraints split cleanly along two axes: H2,
H3, H5, H6, H8 and H9 constrain a single nurse's row, while H4 and H7 constrain
a single day's column. Nothing else couples them, and both algorithms below are
built on that separation.

Letting b_d be the number of B shifts on day d, the column constraints collapse
to one integer per day: the day needs exactly m-b_d nurses on M, a-b_d on A, e
on E and b_d on B, consuming m+a+e-b_d distinct nurses. H4 and H7 are then just
"choose disjoint sets of these sizes". We take b_d as small as H7 allows,
raising it only when a day cannot field m+a+e-b_d distinct nurses, because each
B costs two units of K and forces R or E the next day (H6).

Two consequences are used throughout. First, total workload is invariant:
sum over nurses of (C_M + C_A + C_E) = D*(m+a+e), whatever the b_d. Second,
exchanging two nurses' shifts on one day leaves that day's multiset of shifts
unchanged, so H4 and H7 are preserved automatically.

2. PART A

Since Part A is graded on actual runtime, the solver is layered so the cheap
path runs first.

Pre-search pruning. Six sound necessary conditions reject unsatisfiable
instances immediately, each verified to admit no false positives. The two that
matter most: (i) morning succession -- on any day after the first, the m nurses
who worked a morning are barred by H2 and the e on evenings by H3, so today's m
mornings must come from outside a set of m+e, sharpened per day by how much of
that set can be absorbed by leave; (ii) B capacity over sliding windows -- H2
prevents B on consecutive days, so within W days one nurse covers at most
ceil(W/2) of them, which catches a lone surgical nurse facing two adjacent
surgical days however large K is. A per-nurse DP over H5 and leaves gives an
exact bound on workable days, used for the aggregate checks.

Tier 1: constructive. One randomised greedy pass over days. Within a day the
shift types are filled in MRV order -- least slack (eligible nurses minus slots
needed) first, recomputed after each type since filling one shrinks the others'
pools. Nurses are chosen by an LCV rule with three keys. The first is the
important one: M, B and E are exactly the shifts that bar a morning tomorrow,
and tomorrow always needs m legal mornings, so those shifts go first to nurses
who cannot work tomorrow anyway -- blocking them costs nothing. The remaining
keys prefer the least-spent nurse (keeping K budgets level so nobody exhausts
early and shrinks a later day's pool) and the shortest working run (deferring
H5-forced rests). Randomised tie-breaks make restarting a search strategy.

Tier 2: backtracking. If restarts dead-end, a depth-D search runs, one decision
per day rather than per cell. Each node branches over b_d and over a few
randomised fillings of that day. Forward checking on day d+1 prunes most dead
ends a level early by verifying it retains enough workers, enough nurses with a
legal morning, and enough surgical nurses for its B quota. Bounded randomised
restarts with a geometrically growing node budget counter heavy-tailed runtimes,
since one bad subtree can otherwise absorb the whole budget.

3. PART B

The objective simplifies: 3(x^2+y^2+z^2) - (x+y+z)^2 is identically
(x-y)^2 + (y-z)^2 + (z-x)^2. So a zero-cost nurse has x=y=z, and summing over
nurses forces D*m = D*a = D*e -- cost 0 is attainable only when m = a = e.

Lower bound. Three bounds are computed and the largest taken: Cauchy-Schwarz on
each column, giving C >= D^2[(m-a)^2+(a-e)^2+(e-m)^2]/N; a residue bound from
each nurse's total mod 3; and, where the state space permits, an exact DP over
the relaxation that keeps only H4's column sums and H8's budget. The third
dominates because it sees K: on test3, K=2 means no nurse can ever hold (1,1,1),
so every working nurse costs at least 2 and 18 units need at least 9 of them --
the true optimum is 18, where the analytic bounds suggest 0.

Search. A cost-aware variant of Tier 1 biases each slot toward whichever of
M/A/E that nurse currently has least of, then hill climbing runs over the
same-day swap neighbourhood, which preserves H4 and H7 by construction and needs
only rows d-1, d, d+1 rechecked. Cost is updated by an O(1) delta over the two
nurses touched. Neutral moves are accepted so the search crosses plateaus. The
search stops the moment cost reaches the bound, which on all three sample
instances happens in under two seconds with a proven optimum. Simulated
annealing was implemented and compared head to head; it tied everywhere tested,
so the temperature schedule was dropped rather than carried unused.

4. RESULTS AND OBSERVATIONS

All three samples are solved and verified: Part A in about 0.05s each, Part B at
the proven optimum (36, 24, 18) in 1.6s, 0.15s and 0.08s. On 60 self-generated
instances up to N=50, D=30, Part A returns a valid roster on every satisfiable
one, with the single failure proved unsatisfiable, and Part B lands 2% above the
provable bound in aggregate at only 1.2s per instance.

Two things dominated the effort. Deadline handling: an early version treated a
timeout return as an ordinary branch failure, so unwinding the tree was itself
exponential and a 5s deadline overran to 100s -- fatal under an enforced T. And
diagnosis: every instance the search could not crack turned out to be
unsatisfiable, so the productive work was proving infeasibility rather than
strengthening search. The known remaining limitation is that same-day swaps
preserve b_d, so Part B's local search is confined to the constructed b vector.
